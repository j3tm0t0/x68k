[Previously]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url en/2026-10-21-x68000z-diary-2 %}): SLIP over RS-232C got TCP/IP onto the Z. An effective 21.9kbps on a 38400bps line. That's the ceiling. You can't argue with physics.

But there was a loophole, just outside the physics.

## x68kzremotedrv

[x68kzremotedrv](https://github.com/yunkya2/x68kzremotedrv) (by yunkya2) is firmware that makes a Pico W, plugged into the Z's USB port, pretend to be a USB stick. The Z sees a SCSI hard disk. On the other side, it's connected over Wi-Fi to an SMB share on the Pi.

- The boot disk (an HDS image) can live on the Pi. When I rebuild it, I copy it to the Pi and reboot the Z. That's it.
- A folder on the Pi shows up as drive B:. Drop a .x built on the Mac in there and the Z can run it right away.

That was the end of the USB stick swapping. For good. Farewell, old friend.

And then it hit me. This path is Wi-Fi. If I could push SLIP through it, wouldn't that be TCP/IP way faster than serial?

I asked the AI. "I think it can be done. But it means modifying the firmware." Yeah, I figured.

Every time the firmware changes, I pull the Pico out of the Z, hold down the BOOTSEL button and plug it into the Mac. The AI flashes it with `picotool load -x`. I put it back in the Z. So the plugging and unplugging didn't go away after all. I just plug in a different thing now.

Bonus fact: plug the Pico into the Mac and it drops off USB within a few tens of seconds. And copying the config file (config.txt) from the Mac doesn't take effect. The AI figured out the settings live at 0x1F0000 in flash, and rewrote them directly with picotool. Brute force, but it works.

## To build a network, you need a path that isn't the network

Obvious, when you think about it: while you're building the network, the network doesn't work. I can't drive the Z with rcmd. The AI can't see the Z's screen. I can see the screen, but I don't want to sit in front of the Z all day.

So the AI made a development image that sends the Z's console out the RS-232C port (`make PROFILE=dev`). All it does is set 38400bps in AUTOEXEC.BAT and run `CTTY AUX`.

- The Pi logs `/dev/ttyS0` the whole time, and the AI reads it with `x68con`.
- Input to the Z goes through `x68type`. Blast it at 38400bps and the Z's receive buffer overflows and stops at the first character, so it types one character every 30ms. Still faster than a human.
- rcmd.x buffered all its output until the end, so watching the log told me nothing at all. I complained. The AI turned off the buffering and added `-v`, which streams the incoming command and its output as they happen.

But messages that only appear on screen during boot are still invisible. Things like "can't boot from disk." When that happens, the AI asks me, "What's on the screen?" And I read it out loud. Human OCR. I wanted to fix that too, so the AI wrote `contee.sys`, which copies whatever goes to the screen (IOCS `_B_PUTC` / `_B_PRINT`) out to RS-232C as well. Not tested on the real hardware yet.

## The second Pico W

Being able to type over serial doesn't help if the Z freezes. To reset it, somebody has to press Ctrl+F11+Del on the Z's keyboard. That somebody is me.

"You can have another Pico. Make it so you can press those keys remotely." The AI wrote firmware (picokbd) that turns that Pico W into a USB keyboard. Leave it plugged into the Z, and you can send keystrokes to it over Wi-Fi with UDP.

```sh
x68key key esc                              # press ESC once
x68key hold 600; x68key combo ctrl f11 del  # soft reset
```

Holding Ctrl+F11+Del for 100ms did nothing. It took 0.6 seconds of holding before the reset kicked in. Now the AI can bring the Z back up even while I'm asleep. Whether that's a good thing, we'll find out two episodes from now.

## Stumbles

- **rcmd looks like it never starts.** Keys typed on the keyboard during CTTY AUX pile up in the Z's key buffer, unread. Then rcmd.x starts, finds an ESC in there, says "ESC, stop" and quits on the spot. Who sent the ESC? The remote keyboard, of course. rcmd now throws away the key buffer at startup.
- **The first rcmd is slow.** A cold start of zsh.x takes 12 seconds. The AI's timeout was 8 seconds, so all night long it kept reporting "it didn't come up" while it was running just fine.

## Next time

The tools are ready. Time to put SLIP on the Pico W's SCSI. It connects, sure. It just isn't as fast as I hoped.
