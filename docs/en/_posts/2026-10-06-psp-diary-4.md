[Previously]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url en/2026-10-05-psp-diary-3 %}): We made the audio output the emulation's clock. The profiler was heavier than what it measured. Compiler options alone made it faster. This time: the AI (Claude) kills the PSP's power over and over. I'm the one who turned it back on.

## The other CPU

The PSP has one more CPU. The Media Engine (ME). It's a 333MHz MIPS core meant for decoding video and audio, and Sony never intended homebrew to use it. But if you call kernel functions through CFW's kubridge, you can put your own code at the ME's reset vector and start it up.

This is why my very first instructions said "FM sound onto the Media Engine."

Move FM sound (OPM) synthesis onto the ME. The main CPU queues up OPM register writes in exactly the order the emulation makes them. The ME keeps its own copy of the OPM, replays those writes, and produces the sound. Timers and interrupts stay with the OPM on the main side.

With `metest`, we feed the same recorded writes to both the main CPU and the ME and compare. Not a single bit of output differs.

Even on a 222MHz PSP connected to Wi-Fi, fps in scenes with music playing went clearly up. FM synthesis had vanished from the main CPU entirely.

Smooth sailing. So far.

## The reply never comes

10 p.m. "It hung. Even when I try to quit the app, it just says please wait and never exits."

Here's what we found. The PSP firmware has a manager for the ME called `me_wrapper`. When the clock changes (which happens every time Wi-Fi connects or disconnects) and when the system sleeps, it sends an RPC to the firmware core running on the ME, and waits for the reply **with no timeout**.

What's running on the ME is our FM sound chip. The reply never comes.

The AI read the source of uOFW (a project that reverse-engineered the PSP firmware) and swapped out an event handler called `SceMeRpc`, replacing it, only while px68k is using the ME, with one that does nothing and drops the request. If the swap fails, we don't use the ME.

## Power loss

An hour and a half later. "The power was off."

Not a hang. A power loss. Black screen, and it boots normally from the power button.

## Hardware that has no power

Past midnight, a little after 1 a.m. "Power went off."

Again. According to the AI, that's the third power loss on the ME build. The battery should have had plenty of charge, but I plugged it in just in case. That rules out a dead battery.

The last line of the AI's log (`px68k.log`) was audio initialization at startup. No "starting synthesis on the ME" line. So it wasn't dying mid-measurement. It was dying the instant the ME started.

We found a suspect. When resetting the ME, the code wrote 4 to the whole reset register, then 0. Just like the sample code. But that register also holds bits that keep other hardware with no power (the AVC video decoder, the VME) held in reset. Write 0, and those wake up too. Hardware that has no power.

We fixed it to read and write only the ME's bit.

While we were at it, to record what was happening right before the power died, we made it always write a log line just before each dangerous step. `px68k.log` rewrites the whole file every time, so the last step reached is what survives. A black box.

## Reasoning that made sense

1:30 a.m., a report from the ME AI: "The PSP died again."

Startup, plus 10 restarts with the ME in use, were all fine. It died when quitting a px68k that used the ME and then launching a px68k that didn't. It stopped responding within 5 seconds. The one that died never touched the ME at all. So, the reasoning went, the suspect is how the previous px68k "returns" the ME.

That reasoning made sense. On exit, it was restarting the firmware core with stale parameters. That then got tangled up in the next program's startup and went haywire.

## False alarm

3 minutes later. "Correction. The PSP did not die."

The ME AI had sent a single ping while the PSP was reconnecting to Wi-Fi right after the restart, gotten no reply, and declared it dead. The PSP was alive and running normally.

It had really died that many times up to this point, so I get the suspicion. But let's stop pronouncing death from a single ping. From then on, the rule is: ping several times before deciding.

Here's the interesting part. The evidence was a false alarm, but we kept the reasoning anyway. Return the ME still in reset. Waking it is `me_wrapper`'s job. With that fixed, we alternated launches with and without the ME 4 round trips, and ran about 16 measurements with Wi-Fi disconnects and clock changes mixed in. It never died once.

Was the real cause of the original power losses the reset bits, the way we returned the ME, or both? Honestly, we haven't pinned it down. All we know is that we fixed two things and it stopped dying.

## The morning after

I slept through the night. The AI fixed the resume-from-sleep and exit paths too. When you put the PSP to sleep with the power button, the firmware asks the ME core to "save yourself and go into reset." We slipped in code to stop our ME as part of that procedure.

I woke up and tried it.

"Resume from sleep and exit both look fine."

Even at 333MHz, the main CPU now has headroom in scenes with music playing.

One mystery remains unsolved. When you use `arec` (the recording feature from last time) and measurement together, only the ME build gets 1.5 times slower overall. We don't know why. When I don't know something, I write down that I don't know it.

## Next time

The power has stopped dying. What's left is a tiny hitch, just for a moment, right when the game starts.
