Full disclosure: the code in this series, the research, and even this write-up were mostly done by an AI (Claude). That's normal now. My job is to say what I want, plug and unplug USB sticks, and press the power button.

So what happened? I was setting up a command-line environment on an X68000 Z, and somewhere along the way I ended up with TCP/IP over SLIP over SCSI over USB over Wi-Fi. Yes, really. I'll tell the story in five parts. This is where it starts.

![The X68000 Z, a Raspberry Pi and two Pico Ws]({{ "/assets/setup.jpg" | relative_url }})

This is the work site. The black case on the left holds a Raspberry Pi. The two green boards are Pico Ws, and they become the stars of the second half of this series. One handles the Z's disk and network. The other pretends to be the Z's keyboard. The black sponge keeps the boards from touching the Z's chassis and shorting out. Probably.

## I want a command line

The X68000 Z is a really well-made toy. But Human68k's COMMAND.X is a bit rough by modern standards. I want history. I want completion. I want vim. Ideally make and gcc too.

So I asked the AI: "Make me a Human68k 3.02 HDD image with zsh, vim and the GNU tools, buildable with a single make on my Mac."

The AI went digging through the software archives of the era and came back with this lineup ([x68kcli](https://github.com/j3tm0t0/x68k/tree/main/x68kcli)):

- Shells: zsh 2.5, fish, ksh
- Editor: vim 3.0
- Tools: ITA TOOLBOX, gawk, make, tar, lha, plus GNU sed and grep cross-built with elf2x68k
- Development: gcc (the Mariko build and 2.95), XC

## The image won't boot

The first image didn't boot. Of course it didn't.

- I'd used rb-cli to build the HDS, and Human68k took one look at its FAT, declared "the management area is corrupted," and refused to read it. The AI rewrote the HDS-building part from scratch.
- rb-cli fills the extension field of each directory entry with spaces. Human68k expects zeros, so it can't find HUMAN.SYS. That's it. That one thing is enough to stop the whole boot. The fix: go back after writing and zero them out.
- Put more than 250 files in one directory and things break. That one now gets a warning.

The AI checked the boots itself, by running the XEiJ emulator. It grabs the contents of the text screen and decides whether there's an error on it. The human just watches. What a time to be alive.

## US layout, please

Back when I was using an X68000 for real, I typed on JIS keyboards too. But these days my fingers are wired for US layout. And the X68000 still lives in a JIS world. Every time I try to type `@` and get `"`, a small piece of my soul dies.

The AI brought in KeyWitch.x 2.13, a resident program that remaps keys. It took the bundled ascii.env and built a us.env for a US-layout USB keyboard from it.

Then came a trap specific to the X68000 Z. The `` ` `` / `~` key on a US keyboard reaches the Z as the BREAK scan code. So every time you type it, your program stops. Fun. We swapped BREAK and INS (based on [us_HHKB.env at nharada.com](https://www.nharada.com/programming/x68000z_usHHKB.html)). Losing BREAK entirely would be a problem, so Ctrl+C became BREAK instead.

- Build the image with `make KEYMAP=us`, and CONFIG.SYS loads KeyWitch with the US layout.
- In zsh, `uskey` / `jiskey` switches between them.

Now the keys are where my fingers think they are.

## And then, the USB sticks

Once the image booted, it was time to test on the real hardware. The AI rebuilds the image. I plug a USB stick into the Mac. The AI copies the image over. I unplug it and plug it into the Z. I turn it on. Something's wrong. Back to step one.

In that whole loop, the only job that needs a human is plugging and unplugging the USB stick. And it is, by far, the most annoying part.
